Privacy Policy

Classic Piano for Android (package com.claudev.classicpiano)
Developer: Krisztián Szanyi, Hungary — contact: CONTACT@EXAMPLE.COM
Effective 20 September 2026

The short version. The game has no accounts and no sign-in. It stores your settings and scores on your phone. Four things can send data off the phone: ads (Google AdMob, always on, governed by Google's consent form in the EEA/UK), multiplayer (your nickname and scores, only while you are in a room), run sharing (anonymous timing data, off until you turn it on) and the leaderboard (your nickname and score, published to other players, off until you turn it on).

We do not sell your data, we do not collect your name, e-mail, contacts, location or files, and we never see your advertising ID ourselves.

2. What stays on your phone

Everything the game needs to work is kept locally: your settings, latency calibration, best scores, the nickname you typed, whether the tutorial has been seen, and how many runs you have played (used only to hold back the first ads). None of this is uploaded except as described below, and it is deleted when you uninstall the game.

The game asks for no runtime permissions. It does not access your location, contacts, microphone, camera, photos or files. The only permissions in the app are internet access and the advertising-ID permission that the Google Mobile Ads SDK includes.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows ads through the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) in exactly two places:

  • a rewarded video that you choose to watch, by tapping CONTINUE (WATCH AD) after running out of lives; and
  • an interstitial when you start a new run, never during a song, never in multiplayer, and never in your first few runs after installing.

To serve and measure ads, Google collects data directly from your device. This typically includes your advertising ID, your IP address (from which an approximate location may be inferred), information about your device and this app, and how you interact with the ads shown. This data goes to Google and, depending on your consent choices, to Google's advertising partners. The developer never receives or stores your advertising ID.

How Google uses this data is described in the Google Privacy Policy and in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Your choices about ads

  • Consent form. If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or a US state with an applicable privacy law, the game shows Google's consent form before any ad is requested. You can decline personalised ads there; the game still works, and if no ad can be shown the continue is granted for free. Where the form was shown, an AD PRIVACY OPTIONS button on the game's settings screen reopens it so you can change your answer.
  • Android settings. Under Settings → Google → Ads (or Settings → Privacy → Ads) you can delete or reset your advertising ID and opt out of ad personalisation for every app on the phone.

4. Multiplayer

When you join a multiplayer room, the game connects to a relay server operated by the developer and sends your nickname, the app version, the song chosen, and your live score during the race so the other players in the room can see it. Your IP address is visible to the server for as long as the connection lasts, as with any internet connection.

The relay holds room state in memory only and discards it when the room closes. Its service log records nicknames and match results (for keeping the server working), is not indexed and is rotated automatically by the operating system. IP addresses are not written to the log in normal operation.

Note: the match connection itself is a plain socket, not encrypted. Only your nickname and score travel over it; nothing sensitive is ever sent that way.

5. Run sharing — off until you turn it on

The game's difficulty is a matter of timing, and timing depends on the phone as much as the player. SHARE RUN DATA on the settings screen lets you send the developer one small anonymous summary at the end of each run, so the game can be tuned for phones the developer does not own.

It is off by default, an update can never turn it on, and it is switched on with two taps: the first shows what would be sent, the second agrees to it.

Exactly what is sent

One JSON summary of about 1 KB, over HTTPS, containing:

  • a random install ID generated by the app itself — not your advertising ID, not any hardware identifier — so several runs from one phone can be read together;
  • app version and the time of the run;
  • phone model and Android version; screen refresh rate, audio buffer configuration and touch-input method;
  • which song and difficulty, and under which rules (completed, failed, multiplayer, lives on/off);
  • score, max combo, accuracy, hit counts, and a 21-bin histogram of how early or late your taps were;
  • the latency and approach settings that were in effect.

What is never sent

  • your nickname, or any text you typed;
  • your advertising ID, Android ID, IMEI, MAC address or any cross-app identifier;
  • your location, contacts, accounts, files, microphone or camera;
  • the raw sequence of your individual taps;
  • your IP address, beyond the moment the request is served. The server keeps no access log; a temporary in-memory hash of the address, re-keyed every time the service restarts, is used only for rate limiting and is never written to disk.

Where it goes and how long it stays

To a server rented by the developer from DigitalOcean in the United States (see section 10), stored in a file per run readable only by the developer. Summaries are kept until the tuning question they were collected for is settled, then deleted; they are aggregated, not accumulated.

Withdrawing

Turn the switch off. Uploading stops immediately and the install ID is discarded, so a later opt-in cannot be linked to earlier runs. To have summaries already on the server deleted, contact the developer and say roughly when you played; the ID is random, so that is how the right files are found.

6. Leaderboard — off until you turn it on

The leaderboard shows the top twenty scores per chart to every player. Reading it needs no consent and sends nothing about you. Posting to it is opt-in (POST MY SCORES on the settings screen, two taps, off by default, never turned on by an update), because posting publishes something about you.

Exactly what is published

For each qualifying run: your nickname, your score and your accuracy, visible to everyone who opens the board. A second, separate random ID generated by the app is sent so the server can replace your own earlier entry rather than duplicate it; it is never shown to anyone and is never linked to the run-sharing ID.

Only full clears with lives on are posted. Failed runs, practice runs and multiplayer races never are. Nothing on the "never sent" list in section 5 is sent here either.

Choosing a name

Your nickname is shown to strangers. Please do not use your real name or anything you would not want public. Nicknames are not moderated; the developer can remove an entry by hand on request.

Retention and withdrawing

Leaderboard entries are kept indefinitely — a board that forgets is not a leaderboard — and that is part of what you agree to when you turn posting on. Turning the switch off stops new posts immediately, but entries already published stay up; the switch cannot un-publish them. To have your entries removed, contact the developer and say which nickname.

7. Children

Classic Piano is a music game for a general audience aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has posted a nickname to the leaderboard or turned on run sharing, contact the developer and the data will be removed.

9. Your rights

The developer is based in Hungary, so the GDPR applies. Where the game processes your data itself (multiplayer, run sharing, leaderboard) the legal basis is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time from the settings screen as described above. You have the right to ask what data is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain to a supervisory authority — in Hungary, the Nemzeti Adatvédelmi és Információszabadság Hatóság (NAIH).

Because run sharing is anonymous and the leaderboard is keyed by a random ID, the developer cannot look you up by name or e-mail; requests are matched by nickname or by the time you played, as described in sections 5 and 6.

Data collected by Google for advertising is controlled by Google as an independent controller; use the consent form, your Android ad settings, and Google's My Ad Center for those rights.

10. Security and transfers

Run-sharing summaries and leaderboard traffic are sent over HTTPS. The developer's server is rented from DigitalOcean and is located in the United States (New York). Data sent to it therefore leaves the EEA; the transfer relies on DigitalOcean's data processing agreement, which incorporates the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. The data involved is limited to what sections 4–6 describe: a nickname, scores, and anonymous run summaries.

Google processes advertising data on its own infrastructure, including in the United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and the safeguards described in its privacy policy.

11. Changes to this policy

If what the game collects changes, this page will be updated and the effective date at the top changed. Because run sharing and leaderboard posting are opt-in, an update to the game never turns either on for you.
